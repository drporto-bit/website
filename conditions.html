What virtual care handles well

  • Conditions diagnosed by appearance and historyAcne, rosacea, eczema, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, hives, and most common rashes are identified the same way in person as they are from good photographs and a careful history.
  • Prescription managementNearly every first-line and second-line dermatology medication can be prescribed remotely and sent directly to your pharmacy.
  • Treatment that needs adjusting over timeChronic conditions improve through iteration. Messaging makes it easier to report back at week three than a visit scheduled three months out.
  • Second opinionsIf a treatment has stalled or a diagnosis hasn't held up, a specialist review of what has already been tried is often the fastest way forward.

What still needs an in-person visit

Being straightforward about this matters more than being convenient.

  • Anything requiring a biopsyIf a lesion needs tissue sampled to be diagnosed, that has to happen in person. A virtual visit can tell you whether a biopsy is warranted and how urgently.
  • Surgery, including MohsSkin cancer removal is a procedure. Dr. Porto performs Mohs surgery, but not remotely.
  • Full-body skin cancer screeningA complete skin examination requires a trained eye on the whole surface, often with dermoscopy. Individual spots that worry you can absolutely be reviewed virtually and triaged.
  • Procedural treatmentInjections, lasers, cryotherapy, and excisions all require an in-person appointment.
  • Medications with in-person monitoring requirementsIsotretinoin, for example, requires iPLEDGE enrollment and regular laboratory work, which generally needs a care arrangement that includes in-person testing.
If something is changing

A mole or spot that is growing, changing color, bleeding, or simply new and different from the others deserves attention sooner rather than later. Send a photo and we will tell you honestly whether it needs to be examined in person — and how quickly.
