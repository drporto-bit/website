About

Dennis A. Porto, MD

A double board-certified dermatologist and Mohs micrographic surgeon. He practices in New York and treats patients virtually from coast to coast.

Dr. Porto's training runs through the University of Iowa, Henry Ford Health System in Detroit, and Harvard, and his work included laboratory research at Harvard on how squamous cell carcinoma invades tissue, a co-editorship of the foremost textbook on Botox and other cosmetics in dermatology, and peer-reviewed journal articles cited hundreds of times. He graduated at the top of his class in medical school, matched into a prestigious dermatology residency, completed post-graduate training in Mohs micrographic surgery, graduate school at Harvard, and was awarded the Golden Scalpel from the American Society for Dermatologic Surgery.

Board certification

Dr. Porto holds two certifications from the American Board of Dermatology. The first, in dermatology, was awarded in July 2017. The second, in micrographic dermatologic surgery, was awarded in October 2021.

That second certification is the one worth explaining, because it is uncommon and it is frequently blurred in marketing elsewhere. Mohs micrographic surgery removes skin cancer in stages, with each layer examined under a microscope during the procedure itself, so the surgeon can see exactly where tumor remains before taking any more tissue. It produces the highest cure rates for the most common skin cancers while removing the least healthy skin, and it is why it is the standard for tumors on the face. Formal board certification in it requires post-graduate training beyond a dermatology residency and is held by a small minority of dermatologists.

Practice and teaching

Dr. Porto is a Clinical Assistant Professor at Mount Sinai, where he teaches skin cancer surgery to dermatology residents — the physicians who will spend their careers diagnosing and removing it.

Teaching has run through the whole of his career. He received the Dermatology Resident Teaching Award at Henry Ford, served as invited faculty at the American Society for Dermatologic Surgery’s Surgery Film School, and founded the integrated dermatology–psychiatry clinic at Henry Ford Hospital.

He has practiced full-scope medical and surgical dermatology since 2018. Virtual care through this site is an extension of that work rather than a separate venture — the same physician who trains residents in skin cancer surgery is the one reading your photographs.

Frequently asked questions

Where did Dr. Porto train?

He earned his medical degree at the University of Iowa Carver College of Medicine, where he was inducted into Alpha Omega Alpha as a junior medical student. He completed dermatology residency at Henry Ford Health System in Detroit, a skin cancer research fellowship at the Harvard Skin Disease Research Center, and a Master of Public Health at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health.

What does double board-certified mean?

Dr. Porto holds two separate certifications from the American Board of Dermatology: general dermatology, certified in 2017, and micrographic dermatologic surgery — Mohs surgery — certified in 2021. The second is a subspecialty certification held by a small fraction of dermatologists and requires additional post-graduate training in skin cancer surgery.

Is Dr. Porto a Mohs surgeon?

Yes. Mohs micrographic surgery is a technique for removing skin cancer in stages, examining each layer under the microscope during the procedure so that the smallest possible amount of healthy tissue is removed. Dr. Porto is board-certified in it and performs it as part of his surgical practice.

Has Dr. Porto published research?

He has authored more than fifteen peer-reviewed articles in journals including the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, JAMA Dermatology, Lasers in Surgery and Medicine, and the Journal of Cutaneous Pathology. He is also a co-editor of Botulinum Toxins in Dermatology: Cosmetic and Clinical Applications, published by Wiley-Blackwell.

Does Dr. Porto teach?

Yes. He is a Clinical Assistant Professor at Mount Sinai, where he teaches skin cancer surgery to dermatology residents. He received the Dermatology Resident Teaching Award at Henry Ford Health System in 2017 and has served as invited faculty at the American Society for Dermatologic Surgery's Surgery Film School.

Which states is Dr. Porto licensed in?

New York, Massachusetts, California, Virginia, Maryland, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Hawaii, Pennsylvania, and Connecticut. Each state has its own page describing care available to residents there.
